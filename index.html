Tutorial

  

    
Quick start
A render from SketchUp in five steps. Show me points at the button on screen.

    
    
      
  1. Capture the SketchUp view. With SketchUp open, press the amber Capture SketchUp view button at the top right of the canvas. 
        
    More detailThe view arrives on the canvas as a new picture (SketchUp view; later ones are numbered) and is selected; the next capture goes beside it. Revit works the same way once a Revit has connected (the button then reads Capture view and asks which). Without the plugin, press Add in the canvas toolbar, or drop a screenshot, photo or PDF on the canvas. Ctrl+K → Capture does it too.
    2. 
      
  2. Choose a scene (optional). In the Image tab, under With every render, press Choose a scene… in the Scene row (or press S) and click one, for example Golden hour hero shot. 
        
    More detailThe scene sets the surroundings, sky, light, weather and people, and goes with every render until you change it. Skip this step for a plain daylight render of your view.
    3. 
      
  3. Render. Press the big amber Render button by the prompt (Ctrl+Enter). You don't need to type anything. Your building and camera are kept. 
        
    More detailOn a view with no words, preset or scene, Render adds View to render (Interior render for a room) for a photoreal daylight picture. Add your own words or a preset first if you like. The line under the button says what it will do, for example "on SketchUp view · photoreal daylight". The green Building & camera kept chip means the AI keeps your model and camera as they are.
    4. 
      
  4. Check it. Select the render and open the Compare tab above the picture (C) to see your view and the render side by side. 
        
    More detailSee-through lays your view over the render and Lines only draws its edges in amber, so you can see that nothing moved. For another version, leave the switch under the Render button on From the view: the next render starts from your model again, not from the last render.
    5. 
      
  5. Finish it. Press Upscale ×2 under the picture for a bigger, sharper copy, then Download. 
        
    More detailThe ▾ beside Download chooses with or without the Clarman logo. Or right-click the picture → Add to sheets… to lay it out on an A3 or A1 page, then press Export PDF in the Sheets bar.

Getting started
    
    
      
  1. First, your Google key: the first time VizLab opens, a welcome card asks for the office Google key (ask Stephen; IT makes one at aistudio.google.com → Get API key) and shows whether SketchUp is connected. Paste the key, press Check and save, then Start rendering: a three-step guide waits while you capture a view, press Render and download the result. Show me round first starts the six-step tour instead. The key can be changed later in Settings › Start here; in the desktop app Sign in with Google there can take its place. Take the tour at the top of this page, the ? button at the top right or Ctrl+K shows the tour again at any time.
    2. 
      
  2. Bring in a picture: drag and drop, paste (Ctrl+V), press Add in the canvas toolbar (pictures and PDF drawings), or the amber Capture SketchUp view button. Each one becomes a picture on the canvas with a name that says what it is (SketchUp view 1, house.jpg, Render 2). Rest the mouse on any toolbar button to see its name and key. Settings › Preferences › Show labels on toolbars (on at first) puts a word under every toolbar icon on the canvas, in Draw and in Touch-ups.
    3. 
      
  3. The left sidebar is in three groups. Work: Render (the canvas, where you make pictures), Sheets and Library. Files: Projects and History. Help & settings, at the bottom: this Tutorial, Usage and Settings. This month's estimate at the top right (for example ~$3.40 this month, shown once something has been made) also opens Usage, and the ? beside it opens help. On a narrow window the sidebar becomes a row along the top, with a thin line between the groups.
    4. 
      
  4. The big button: select a picture, pick a preset or type a prompt if you like, then press the big amber button by the prompt (Ctrl+Enter). It says what it will do: Render for a SketchUp or Revit view, Edit for a render or photo, Render 4 views with several views selected, Create in the New picture tool, Upscale in the Upscale tool, Make video or Make 3D model in the other tabs. The small line under it names the picture it works on ("on SketchUp view 2", "edits house.jpg · rest of the photo kept"), so you can see before you press it whether the next version starts from your model or from the last render. Results appear as new pictures joined to the one they came from, so you can branch and try again.
    5. 
      
  5. The Image tab: the right panel has three tabs, Image, Video and 3D & CAD (Alt+1, Alt+2, Alt+3), and each keeps its own prompt. At the top of Image, a switch picks the tool: Render / edit (renders a SketchUp or Revit view, edits any other picture), New picture (from your words alone) or Upscale. Tools beside it holds Batch render…, Make elevation…, Cut out an object…, True elevation…, Photomontage… and Describe this picture for me…, and at the foot Drawings & 3D tools, which opens the 3D & CAD tab. Under it, With every render lists the scene, Project look and Furniture & materials (see Rendering). Then one settings line sums up how the next picture is made, for example Nano Banana 2.1 · 2K · same shape as the view · 1 version: press Settings ▾ on it to open the AI model, Resolution, Aspect ratio and Versions (how many pictures each press gives). Then the presets. The whole panel scrolls, while the tabs and the preset search stay at the top. VizLab always opens on Render / edit.
    6. 
      
  6. Projects and saving: your work saves itself. The title bar shows the project's name and whether it is Saving…, Saved or Not saved · Try again. Click the name for the project menu: Name this project… (or Rename…), New project, Save now (Ctrl+S), your recent projects and All projects…. A new project takes the SketchUp model's name at the first capture (the message has Rename), and naming the project title on Sheets names the project too. Projects in the sidebar lists them all, with a search box, a sort and Rename, Duplicate and Delete on each card. Settings › About › Back up all projects makes one file of every project, its pictures and sheets, History and your own presets; Restore from a backup… adds them back.
    7. 
      
  7. SketchUp (2016 or later): install the VizLab plugin and keep SketchUp open. VizLab connects by itself (Settings › SketchUp & Revit shows the status). The amber Capture SketchUp view button at the top right of the canvas captures the SketchUp view (with Revit connected too it reads Capture view and asks which). On a 3D model, Send to SketchUp converts it to a .dae file at the height you choose; then click in SketchUp to place it. The other way round, Send 3D to VizLab on SketchUp's VizLab toolbar sends your selection (or the whole model) here as a 3D model: orbit it and press Use this view to render that angle.
    8. 
      
  8. Learning VizLab: the Quick start at the top of this page is a whole render in five steps (each step's Show me points at the button). Take the tour shows the main controls in six steps, with five more tips at the end. Start rendering on the welcome card runs the First render guide: three small cards that wait while you capture a view, press Render and download the result. The ? button at the top right has the Quick start, Search help, Keyboard shortcuts, the tour and What's new; Ctrl+K finds every help topic as How to: …, and ? lists every key.
    9. 
      
  9. What next? With one picture selected, the bar under the picture suggests the next steps for that kind of picture, at most five, and never one that already has a button on screen. A SketchUp or Revit view: Render it, Choose a scene…, Try materials, Render all 4 views (with several on the canvas) and Make elevation…. A render: Fix one area…, Update the design…, Compare with Render 1 (the version before), Make a video, Make 3D model, Try materials and Add to sheets…. A photo: Change part of it, Change colour (RAL)…, Remove cars & clutter, Photomontage: put the proposal in… and Describe this picture for me…. An elevation or drawing: Convert to CAD (DXF)… first. Cut-outs, videos and 3D models get steps of their own, and a picture with Draw layers offers Save as new picture and Compare before and after. The × hides the bar; Settings › Preferences › What next? suggestions brings it back.
    10. 
      
  10. Costs: the figure under the big button is a rough estimate before you press it, and the figure at the top right is this month's estimate for this PC (click it for Usage). Work through the Google key uses Google's published prices (e.g. Nano Banana 2.1 is about $0.03 at 1K and $0.05 at 2K). The lowest resolution, 1K (press Settings ▾ on the settings line in the Image tab), is the quickest and cheapest for drafts. With a client watching the screen, switch off Settings › Preferences › Show running costs: both figures go (Usage keeps them).
    11. 
    

    

    
Rendering
    
    
      
  1. Keep my SketchUp camera and model: when you render a SketchUp or Revit view, a green chip by the prompt says Building & camera kept: the AI is told to keep every wall, roof, window and door exactly where it is and not to move the camera, whatever scene or preset you use. Scenes and environments then change only the sky, light, weather, surroundings and people. A Camera preset (Low angle, Drone…) turns the chip amber: the AI redraws the building from that viewpoint, so details can change. For an exact building from another angle, move the camera in SketchUp and capture again. A Change preset (extension, new colours…) unlocks it on purpose (grey chip). Click the chip to switch the lock on or off; the same switch is in the Scene builder's Camera step. A new capture sets Aspect ratio back to Same as the view (a note says so if it was something else), though a Batch render keeps the shape you chose. If you then choose a different shape, a note under the settings line warns that the AI will re-frame your view and the building may shift: click Use the same shape as the view in it and crop afterwards.
    2. 
      
  2. With every render: under the tool switch in the Image tab, three rows say what goes with each render besides your words: Scene, Project look and Furniture & materials, each with what is in use and Choose… (Set up… for the look; Change… once something is set). The same three show as amber chips by the prompt (Scene: name, Look: name, Library). The × on a row or a chip means the same everywhere: leave it out of the next render only. Nothing is deleted or unticked; the chip stays crossed out until that render is sent, and a click brings it back at once. To drop the scene for good, choose No scene in the Scene panel (or Ctrl+K → No scene). In Draw, a change made as a new layer sends only your words, so the chips grey out.
    3. 
      
  3. From the view or from this picture: with a render selected, a small switch under the Render button chooses where the next one starts. From the view (the usual choice for a render, a new picture or an upscale) makes a fresh version from your SketchUp or Revit view with the current words, scene, look and Library, so changes don't pile up from version to version (it would look as if the model drifts); the button reads Render and the line names the view. From this picture (the usual choice for a picture with Draw notes, layers or a material map) edits the picture itself, for example to change one thing in it. The switch remembers your click for that picture until you select another. Right-click a render → Again from the SketchUp view (or Ctrl+K) selects the view it was made from, even several steps back, and keeps your prompt, scene and settings.
    4. 
      
  4. Several views at once: select two or more SketchUp or Revit views (Shift-click them, or Ctrl+K → Select all SketchUp views) and the button reads Render 4 views: one render each, with the same words, scene and look (View to render when the prompt is empty). The views stay selected for the next round. When the selection also holds pictures that are not views, they all go into one picture as inputs instead, for example a view with a moodboard as references (Ctrl+K → Render with the others as references does that for views too). For video, the second selected picture is the last frame.
    5. 
      
  5. Editing a photo: select a photo (or a render) and press Edit. With Edits of a photo or render keep the rest of the picture ticked (under Settings ▾ on the settings line; the same switch as Keep the rest in Draw), everything outside what changed stays exactly as it was; the line under the button says so, for example "edits house.jpg · rest of the photo kept". An edit that changes most of the picture on purpose ("make it night") is kept whole. The result is named after the photo (house – edited).
    6. 
      
  6. Project look: press Set up… in the Project look row under With every render (or L), select your approved render on the canvas and press Use the selected image (or right-click the render → Edit picture › Use as the project look…), add the materials (RAL codes work) and save; the switch at the top turns it on or off (off keeps it for later). Time of day, weather and season are folded under Fix the time and weather for every view: a scene already sets them, so choose them only when every view in the set must share one. Pick a style board… opens the Library's Style boards (what you typed is kept): choose a board and press Use as the project look to come back with it. While it is on (the row reads its name and an amber Look chip shows by the prompt) every render in this project copies that image's materials, colours, light and finish but keeps its own building and camera, so a set of views matches. It is saved with the project.
    7. 
      
  7. New picture (the tool switch at the top of the Image tab) makes a new picture from a description alone; its button reads Create (with a new picture from your words under it). Any images you select are used as style references only: with a SketchUp or Revit view selected an amber note under the button warns that it ignores your building's geometry. Press it anyway and VizLab asks: Render my view switches to Render / edit, New picture anyway carries on.
    8. 
      
  8. AI models that need a key: in the AI model menus (in the Image tab press Settings ▾ on the settings line to see it), models whose service key isn't saved yet are greyed out at the end of the list with what they need, for example "needs a fal.ai key (Settings)" for Seedream, FLUX, the upscalers and most video and 3D models, or "needs a Tripo key". They can't be picked until the key is saved in Settings (the Google key under Start here, fal.ai and Tripo under More AI services; Need a key in the menu opens the right place); then they come back by themselves (with your last choice). Nano Banana needs only the Google key. If no model in the list can run yet, the settings line says what is needed in amber and stays open.
    9. 
      
  9. A4, A3 and A2 sheets: in the Image tab press Settings ▾ on the settings line, open Aspect ratio and pick a paper size under Paper (landscape or portrait). The AI makes the picture at 3:2, the nearest shape it offers, and VizLab trims a little off the sides (or top and bottom) so it is exactly the sheet's shape. The download is a PNG that already knows its print size, so Photoshop or the printer puts it on the sheet edge to edge. Rest the mouse on the picture on the canvas to see the dpi it prints at: 4K is plenty for A4, while A3 and A2 usually need Upscale to reach 300 dpi (an upscale keeps the sheet size). A new SketchUp or Revit capture sets the shape back to Same as the view, so pick the paper size after capturing (a Batch render keeps the shape you chose).
    10. 
      
  10. Batch render: type the prompt (or pick a preset, or leave it empty for View to render) and press Tools → Batch render… in the Image tab. Under What to render choose SketchUp scenes (each saved scene), Revit views (3D views, elevations, sections, plans) or Selected pictures, tick the ones you want, set Versions per view (1 to 4) and press Render (or Enter). Each one is captured and rendered on its own with the same prompt and scene, AI model, resolution, picture shape and Project look, and the set is grouped on the canvas in a grid. A batch keeps the shape you chose under Aspect ratio (a paper size too), while a single new capture sets it back to Same as the view. Your SketchUp view is put back as it was afterwards; Revit exports the views without leaving the one you're in.
    11. 
    

    

    
Scenes
    
    
      
  1. Scenes (one click): the Scene row under With every render in the Image tab reads Scene: none. Choose a scene… there (or S) opens the Scene panel (on the kind of scene you last used in this project, or Interior while Library furniture is in your renders). Type in Find a scene at the top (a name or a mood: dusk, rain, snow, night, sunny, mist), or move between the tiles with the arrow keys and press Enter. There are 39 ready-made scenes in Exterior (planning street view, golden hour hero, blue hour, after the rain, busy high street, Mournes, coastal, aerial, elevated, frost, snow, fog, storm…), Interior (Nordic living, contemporary living, family kitchen, dinner party, bedroom, spa bathroom, office, café, garden room…) and Product, with their example pictures; the current one is marked. One click sets the scene: surroundings, sky, light, weather, people and the finish, and for interiors its furniture collection goes into your renders from the Library. The scene does not go into the prompt box: it shows in the Scene row and as an amber chip by the prompt (Scene: Golden hour hero shot; rest the mouse on it to read it) and is added after your own words when you press Render, so your words stay short and changing the scene is one click. The × on the chip or the row leaves the scene out of the next render only; No scene in the panel clears it (its message has Undo). A new scene replaces the last one, and the furniture the last scene put in goes out with it (the message names it; Undo puts it all back). The same scenes are in the Scene builder's Start from and Ctrl+K (Scene: … sets one straight away, a mood word such as dusk finds them, Scenes… opens the panel). Your own saved scenes come first, under Your scenes (⋯ renames or deletes one). Until a scene has an example picture, its tile shows its light and weather. An interior scene's furniture comes from the Starter libraries (Draw the furniture… in the panel); if your own products are already in renders, the scene leaves its furniture out and offers Add the scene's furniture too, and at most 6 pieces go with each render (the message names any left out). Adjust opens a scene in the Scene builder first; Build your own scene… starts the builder. Each render keeps its scene: select it and the Scene row says which scene it was made with, and Use prompt brings back both. After a new SketchUp or Revit capture the Scene row pulses while no scene is set. Draw layer changes (a selected area in Draw) and drawing briefs (From drawings, Line drawings for CAD and the other briefs) do not use the scene: the chip greys out while one is in use. Example pictures… draws a picture of each scene (and of every builder option) with your image AI model; Every option by picture at the top of the panel shows the builder options as pictures. Choosing a scene takes any scene text written into the prompt box out of it.
    2. 
      
  2. Scene builder (build your own scene): in the Scene panel (Choose a scene…, or Change… once a scene is set, in the Scene row) press Build your own scene…, or Adjust on a scene to start from it (Shift+S adjusts the current scene; Ctrl+K → Scene builder). Presets that describe an environment (light, weather, sky, setting, people, camera, activities…) have a small layers button in their corner on hover: it opens the builder with that preset added as the matching layer, on top of what you already have. Choose Exterior, Interior or Product at the top: each has its own steps, ready-made sets and realism check. Interior: space & style, the view out of the windows, daylight & time, sun through the windows, lamps, finishes, styling, activity, camera, grade. Product: setting, surface, key light, accent lights, atmosphere, props, interaction (hands, people using it), camera, grade. Every mode has an Activity layer, and the same activities are presets in the Interaction & activity groups of the Exterior, Interior and Product tabs. Exterior mode: start from one of the scenes if you like (Start from lists the same scenes as the Scene panel: Wet November evening, Misty Mourne morning, City heatwave…; it folds to one line once you are choosing layers), then work down the steps like a VFX artist: 1 Surroundings, 2 Sky (cloud type and cover), 3 Atmosphere & effects (stack fog, haze, heat shimmer, rain, god rays… each subtle, noticeable or strong), 4 Light, 5 Sun position (drag the sun on the dome, or click in your picture to put the sun in the shot; a live 3D preview shows the real shadows, on your own design if a 3D model is selected; the prompt then says which faces are lit and how long the shadows are), 6 Materials & surfaces (wet, puddles, frost, snow…), 7 Camera (with Keep my SketchUp camera and model on, say there what kind of view yours is: Street level, Elevated or Aerial; with it off, that choice sits at the top), 8 Life, 9 Activity, 10 Colour grade & finish. Options that can't happen with your other choices are greyed out (hover one to see why: heat shimmer with snow, sunbeams at night, a single spotlight in a white studio…), and things that naturally go together are added for you with a short note (rain adds wet surfaces, falling snow adds snow cover, golden hour lowers the sun). Every category has a Clear button (and an × in the list on the left) to put it back to as it is. Press Use this scene to make it the scene for your renders (the chip by the prompt; it is named after the ready-made set you started from, or Custom scene once you change it), or Save as my scene… to keep it in Scenes under Your scenes (and under Start from). See the prompt it writes sits beside the buttons, which always stay in view.
    3. 
      
  3. Hover to enlarge: move the mouse onto any catalogue thumbnail (Library items, collections, scenes, example pictures, the Furniture & materials window) and a large version grows out of it straight away; slide along the row to flick through them with its name, so you can see the detail before you use it. It disappears when you move off or click.
    4. 
    

    

    
Presets and the prompt
    
    
      
  1. Presets: a click picks one preset, replacing the previous one (anything you typed yourself is kept). To combine several, tick the small box in a tile's top-right corner: after that, every preset you click is added (or taken out again), numbered in order. Untick them all, or clear the prompt with ×, to go back to picking one.
    2. 
      
  2. Preset categories: Exterior, Interior, Product, Landscape and Montage, plus All and My presets. Drawing presets sit at the bottom of Exterior and Interior ("From drawings"): drop a PDF or image of an elevation, section, plan or site plan on the canvas (each PDF page becomes a picture), select it and pick one. Landscape plans are under Landscape → Plans. Group headings fold open and shut: click a heading (its arrow turns) to show or hide its presets, and VizLab remembers which are open in each category. At first Render and Light & time (Interior: Light) are open, or the first two groups where a category has no Render group. A folded heading shows how many presets it holds, with an amber tag when one of them is in the prompt. A search shows every match whatever is folded, and All shows every preset in one grid.
    3. 
      
  3. Finding presets: the search box above the preset tabs (/ jumps to it) searches every preset in every category, by its name, its words and its old name. Hover a preset and click its ☆ to make it a favourite. One Yours strip at the top of every category holds your saved combos, favourites, recent combos and recently used presets, each with a small label (Combo, Favourite, Recent combo, Recent); when there are more than fit, the arrows at its ends move along (on a touchpad, swipe sideways).   1. Right-click a picture (or long-press it on a touch screen) for its menu.   2. Several pictures selected: a bar along the top of the canvas says how many and offers what fits them, such as Compare the two, Add to sheets, Quick PDF and Download all; its × clears the selection (Esc too). Ctrl+click or Shift+drag adds pictures to the selection.
    3. 
      
  3. Keeping a big graph tidy: press Tidy up in the canvas toolbar (or T) to lay everything out neatly left to right, each picture followed by what was made from it, long runs of versions wrapped into columns (Ctrl+Z puts it back). The mini-map in the bottom corner shows the whole graph: click or drag in it to jump around (toolbar button to hide it).     4. 
      
  4. Command bar: press Ctrl+K (or the search box at the top) and type anything: a preset (dusk, people, kitchen), a tool (tidy, batch, true elevation, touch-ups), a scene or a mood, a project, a page (settings, history) or words from a picture's prompt. The results are grouped under small headings (Selected picture, Actions, Scenes, Presets, Collections, Projects, Pictures on the canvas, How to), the best match first, each with its key where it has one.   5. Messages and Activity: messages appear above the prompt bar, up to three at once (at the top right of the picture in Draw and Touch-ups, and at the top while a window is open).   6. History: History in the sidebar lists everything made, newest first, as large cards with the title, project and date. Choose All projects or This project, a kind (renders and edits, upscales, videos, 3D models, 3D worlds) or ★ Favourites, or search. Click a card to see it full screen (← → step, F favourite, Esc closes); Add to canvas brings it back into the open project (one already there is selected, not copied). Show older loads more, and Open the save folder opens the folder Settings saves to.
